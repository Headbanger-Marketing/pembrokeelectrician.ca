A symptom is a starting point, not a diagnosis

Loss of power, flickering, recurring protective-device trips or a damaged device can have different causes. The useful information is what happened, where and when, not a guess made from equipment age. A Licensed Electrical Contractor should assess the installation within a defined diagnostic scope and explain the identified cause before repair work is approved.

Prepare the scope for your Pembroke property

Write down the location, the item plugged in and the moment you first saw a mark, felt warmth or heard a hum. Take a photograph of the plate as it is and leave everything on the wall untouched.

Ask whether the contractor will trace what else shares that circuit, and how any repair someone made earlier will be handled in the findings. Ask to receive the cause in plain words before agreeing to repair work.

What should the assessment establish?

A device in a Pembroke home shows heat or a change of colour. The circuit feeding it is identified, and what the contractor actually sees is recorded, with no cause inferred from how old the fittings look. Any signs that someone altered the wiring in the past are written up, as are the parts that stayed out of sight. Any outbuilding on the same feed is named as included or excluded. Before any repair is proposed, the cause is given in everyday language, and the report keeps confirmed findings apart from open ones.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Can I leave a scorched-looking wall plate in use until someone visits?

A hot or discoloured plate is a cue to stop using whatever is connected and leave it alone. Do not open it or probe it yourself; describe what you noticed to a contractor, who can assess it.

My switch only works sometimes: is that worth an assessment?

Yes. An unreliable switch may be the device itself or wiring that someone altered earlier, and from the outside the two look the same. Keep a short log of when it fails and what else on that circuit acts oddly.

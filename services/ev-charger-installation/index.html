EV charger installation in Pembroke.

Adding a charger to a Pembroke property in the same year as a new heat pump or workshop heater means several new loads may share one supply. Undocumented earlier work makes that total harder to judge.

The charger is only one part of the installation

An EV charging project includes equipment, a suitable connection and an assessment of the property supply. ESA advises using approved charging equipment, a dedicated circuit and outdoor-rated equipment where needed. The contractor should evaluate the proposed load and explain whether the supply, a load-management arrangement or another design is suitable. Do not choose a circuit from the charger advertising alone.

Prepare the scope for your Pembroke property

List every new load planned this year, such as the charger, a heater or a workshop tool, and take photographs of the panel door and meter position from the outside. Note any earlier additions you know about.

Ask whether the proposal counts the other new loads together with the charger, and what it assumes about circuits you cannot document. Ask where the charger cable will run and who confirms that route.

What should the assessment establish?

Priced for the charger alone and silent on the other equipment planned that year, a quote leaves a Pembroke homeowner unable to tell which loads it allowed for. Instead, the combined demand of everything planned is worked out. The existing supply and panel are recorded, any part that earlier work left undocumented is flagged, and the contractor explains whether load management belongs in the design. The dedicated circuit, outdoor-rated parts where needed and the ESA notification appear as items. If the charger would go in a detached garage, the scope states whether the existing feeder is reused or replaced, and why.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance, and EV charging safety guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Does adding a charger change what else I can add this year?

It can, because the charger and other new equipment draw on the same supply. A contractor can assess everything planned together and say what the result leaves for later additions.

Can a garage with an old feed from the house take a charger?

It rests on what the feed is and whether anyone can document it, which only an assessment can establish. Do not assume an existing garage circuit suits charging because it already powers lights or tools.
